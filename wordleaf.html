Wordleaf

Write with your voice.

Wordleaf is a completely free desktop writing program built around dictation. Use your voice to write, format a page, add or move pictures, reorganize a section, work on an outline, or tell the document what you want changed.

It pays attention to context and intention. A sentence can be part of your manuscript, a quotation, a thought you are still working through, or an instruction to the document. Wordleaf is designed to tell the difference instead of treating everything you say like a command.

Your words stay yours

Wordleaf can clean up dictation and reorganize material you already gave it. It does not quietly replace your writing, invent missing facts, or fill in blank sections unless you explicitly ask it to create something new.

Format by speaking

Ask for headings, lists, tables, captions, page breaks, columns, margins, headers, footers, page numbers, fonts, spacing, alignment, references, a table of contents, or image placement.

Structured documents

Work with tables, figures, equations, citations, cross-references, diagrams, charts, timelines, Gantt charts, glossaries, acronym lists, units, and chemistry formulas when the document calls for them.

Outline help, not outline replacement

Wordleaf can help place your existing ideas into structures such as the Hero's Journey or a 27-point story structure, show which beats already have material, point out gaps, and help you think about order. It does not fill the empty beats with its own story.

Essays and research papers

Use Wordleaf to organize and format essays, reports, theses, research papers, notes, references, tables, figures, and citations. It can work with APA, MLA, Chicago/Turabian, IEEE, AMA, and common report conventions. It does not invent sources or research data.

Books and publishing

Choose named formats such as B1, B2, B3, B4, A4, and US Letter, or use book-oriented page presets. Export a finished PDF for publishing or self-publishing, including workflows where you are preparing a book for Amazon KDP.

Frequently asked questions

What can Wordleaf help with?

These answers are intentionally specific. Wordleaf helps organize, format, and work with material you provide. It does not fill missing ideas, facts, research, story beats, or sources unless you explicitly ask it to create new material.

Can Wordleaf help me write a fiction book?

Yes. You can dictate scenes, chapters, dialogue, notes, character information, and story ideas, then ask Wordleaf to help organize what you already have. It can help map your material to structures such as three-act structure, five-act structure, Freytag’s Pyramid, the Hero’s Journey, the 27-point story structure, seven-point story structure, Story Circle, Story Spine, Fichtean Curve, quest structure, episodic structure, circular structure, nonlinear structure, parallel narrative, frame narrative, mystery/reveal structure, romance arcs, tragedy arcs, coming-of-age arcs, and scene-sequel organization. It can show where your existing scenes fit and where there are empty beats. It does not write the missing beats for you unless you explicitly ask it to create new material.

Can Wordleaf help with the Hero’s Journey?

Yes. Give Wordleaf the scenes, events, notes, character changes, or chapter ideas you already have. It can help place those ideas against stages such as the ordinary world, call to adventure, refusal, mentor, threshold, tests, ordeal, reward, return, and transformation. If a stage has no material, Wordleaf can point that out or ask you a question. It does not invent the missing event for you.

Can Wordleaf help with a 27-point story structure?

Yes. Wordleaf can help sort your existing ideas across the 27-point framework, identify which points already have scenes or notes attached, and show which points are still empty. It can help you move scenes or ideas into a different order. Empty points remain empty until you decide what belongs there.

What other fiction story structures can it help organize?

Wordleaf can use common structural references including three-act and five-act structure, Freytag’s Pyramid, seven-point story structure, Story Circle, Story Spine, Fichtean Curve, Hero’s Journey, Heroine’s Journey when requested, Save the Cat beat-sheet concepts, in medias res, frame stories, nested stories, parallel narratives, nonlinear narratives, episodic stories, circular stories, quests, mystery/reveal structures, romance arcs, tragedy arcs, character arcs, coming-of-age arcs, and scene-sequel organization. These are ways to organize your own material, not automatic story generators.

Can Wordleaf help me make a children’s book or picture book?

Yes. Wordleaf can help organize text and images across pages or spreads and can work with structures such as beginning-middle-end, problem-attempt-resolution, cumulative or repetitive stories, circular stories, journeys and quests, bedtime progressions, concept books, alphabet books, counting books, question-and-answer books, compare-and-contrast books, cause-and-effect books, and patterned or refrain-based books. You can add images, place them in the document, and move them. Wordleaf does not invent the child’s story, characters, moral, or missing page text unless you explicitly ask it to.

Can Wordleaf help me organize an essay?

Yes. It can help organize material you already have into structures such as a five-paragraph essay, argumentative essay, persuasive essay, expository essay, analytical essay, compare-and-contrast essay, cause-and-effect essay, problem-solution essay, definition essay, process or how-to essay, narrative essay, reflective essay, rhetorical analysis, literary analysis, synthesis essay, position paper, or response paper. It can help with headings, paragraph order, citations, references, tables, figures, and formatting without inventing your argument or evidence.

Can Wordleaf help with a research paper?

Yes. It can help organize a research paper, literature review, annotated bibliography, research proposal, thesis, dissertation, lab report, position paper, or IMRaD paper with Introduction, Methods, Results, and Discussion sections. It can organize sources and citation information you provide, format references, and help place tables or figures. It is explicitly told not to invent studies, authors, quotations, statistics, methods, results, page numbers, DOIs, URLs, or references.

What citation and writing styles can Wordleaf work with?

Wordleaf has built-in guidance for APA, MLA, Chicago and Turabian, IEEE, AMA, and common business and report conventions. It can help with headings, citations, reference lists, notes, bibliography structure, captions, cross-references, and document organization. If a particular edition matters, the source information still needs to come from you or from a source you provide.

Can Wordleaf help with nonfiction books?

Yes. It can help organize nonfiction material using chronological, thematic, topical, problem-solution, question-and-answer, how-to or step-by-step, reference/manual, case-study, memoir, biography, braided narrative, collection, and progressive chapter structures. It reorganizes the material you provide rather than manufacturing missing expertise or facts.

Can I use my voice for formatting, not just dictation?

Yes. Wordleaf is designed so voice can be used for document instructions as well as writing. You can ask for headings, lists, tables, captions, page breaks, columns, margins, headers, footers, page numbers, fonts, spacing, alignment, table-of-contents changes, references, image placement, and other document changes. The dictation layer uses context to distinguish ordinary writing from a command.

Can Wordleaf add and move pictures?

Yes. Wordleaf has an image library for local PNG, JPG, JPEG, WebP, GIF, and SVG files. Images can be inserted into the document and included in PDF export. Voice or document instructions can be used to request placement and document changes around images, while the original image files remain part of the project assets.

Can Wordleaf make charts, diagrams, timelines, or technical material?

Wordleaf has built-in knowledge for document features and packages that support diagrams, charts, graphics, arrow and node diagrams, timelines, Gantt charts, glossaries, abbreviation and acronym lists, SI units, equations, chemistry formulas, tables, figures, citations, cross-references, presentations, and technical documentation. These features are used when the project actually needs them rather than being added automatically.

What page sizes can Wordleaf use?

Wordleaf supports named page formats including B1, B2, B3, B4, A4, and US Letter, along with book-oriented presets. B1, B2, B3, and B4 are real ISO B-series paper formats. B4 is often a more practical example for ordinary document work than the much larger B1 or B2 sizes. The selected page size is used by the live preview and PDF export.

Can Wordleaf make a publishing-ready PDF?

Yes. Wordleaf has a live PDF workflow and can export the current project as a finished PDF using the selected page profile. You can control page size and ask for document formatting such as margins, headers, footers, page numbers, spacing, headings, images, tables, figures, and other layout details before exporting.

Can I use the PDF for Amazon KDP or Kindle Direct Publishing?

Wordleaf can create a finished PDF for a book workflow, including projects intended for Amazon Kindle Direct Publishing (KDP) or other print and self-publishing services that accept PDF interiors. Wordleaf prepares the document; you still choose the publisher’s trim size, bleed, margins, cover requirements, metadata, and upload settings and should check the current requirements of the publishing service before submission.

Can I make a book in Wordleaf instead of Microsoft Word?

Yes. Wordleaf is intended for book-length projects and combines dictation, organization, document formatting, images, page layout, live PDF preview, project saving, and PDF export. If you already started in Microsoft Word, you can import a .docx file and continue working in Wordleaf.

Can I move a Google Docs book into Wordleaf?

Yes. Export the Google Docs document as a Word .docx file and import that file into Wordleaf. Wordleaf also accepts plain-text and Markdown files. This lets you continue a manuscript that began in Google Docs without copying every section by hand.

Exactly what files can Wordleaf import?

Wordleaf currently imports Microsoft Word .docx files, Google Docs documents exported as .docx, plain-text .txt files, Markdown .md files, native Wordleaf project files, and compatible project folders. Images can be added separately in PNG, JPG, JPEG, WebP, GIF, and SVG formats. Wordleaf does not currently advertise direct PDF-to-editable-document import.

What can Wordleaf export?

Wordleaf can export a finished PDF and can export the complete editable Wordleaf project so the manuscript, assets, settings, writing rules, and related project data can travel together and be reopened later. A separate plain-text export is not currently advertised.

Does Wordleaf rewrite my writing automatically?

No. Its rules specifically tell it to preserve your meaning, voice, terminology, intentional fragments, dialect, and style. It may clean speech-recognition artifacts or reorganize material when you ask, but substantive rewriting is supposed to happen only when you explicitly request it.

Will Wordleaf fill in missing story beats or blank outline sections for me?

No, not by default. “Organize” means arrange, group, label, map, or move your existing material. If a Hero’s Journey stage, 27-point beat, essay section, research section, chapter, or placeholder is empty, Wordleaf can identify the gap and help you think about it, but it is explicitly told not to invent the missing content unless you ask it to create something new.

Will Wordleaf invent research, citations, or facts?

No. Its internal rules explicitly prohibit inventing research findings, statistics, quotations, citations, sources, names, dates, page numbers, references, story beats, examples, or other data unless you specifically ask for fictional or example material. For real research, the evidence and sources must come from you or from material you provide.

Is Wordleaf an AI writer?

Wordleaf includes local language tools to understand dictation, context, organization, and document instructions, but its design is not “press a button and replace the author.” It is built to work with your words and your ideas, make the smallest requested change, and avoid silently filling in missing content.

Can Wordleaf help me figure out how to write a book?

It can help you take what you already know about the book and make it easier to work with: collect notes, dictate ideas, group related material, build and revise an outline, compare your material with common story structures, format chapters, add images, and prepare the final document. It will not decide what your book should be about or invent the missing book for you.

Can Wordleaf help with reports, resumes, letters, proposals, or technical documents?

Yes. Its built-in document knowledge covers books, chapters, manuscripts, essays, research papers, reports, proposals, resumes and CVs, letters, business documents, technical documentation, lab reports, presentations, posters, flyers, notes, and theses. Formatting and organization still follow the material and instructions you provide.

Is Wordleaf free?

Yes. Wordleaf is completely free to use. The Windows download is available now. A macOS build is planned and will be added when there is a real tested Mac release to download.
