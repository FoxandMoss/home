Wordleaf

Write with your voice.

Wordleaf is a completely free desktop writing program built around dictation. Use your voice to write, format a page, add or move pictures, reorganize a section, work on an outline, or tell the document what you want changed.

It pays attention to context and intention. A sentence can be part of your manuscript, a quotation, a thought you are still working through, or an instruction to the document. Wordleaf is designed to tell the difference instead of treating everything you say like a command.

Your words stay yours

Wordleaf can clean up dictation and reorganize material you already gave it. It does not quietly replace your writing, invent missing facts, or fill in blank sections unless you explicitly ask it to create something new.

Format by speaking

Ask for headings, lists, tables, captions, page breaks, columns, margins, headers, footers, page numbers, fonts, spacing, alignment, references, a table of contents, or image placement.

Structured documents

Work with tables, figures, equations, citations, cross-references, diagrams, charts, timelines, Gantt charts, glossaries, acronym lists, units, and chemistry formulas when the document calls for them.

Outline help, not outline replacement

Wordleaf can help place your existing ideas into structures such as the Hero's Journey or a 27-point story structure, show which beats already have material, point out gaps, and help you think about order. It does not fill the empty beats with its own story.

Essays and research papers

Use Wordleaf to organize and format essays, reports, theses, research papers, notes, references, tables, figures, and citations. It can work with APA, MLA, Chicago/Turabian, IEEE, AMA, and common report conventions. It does not invent sources or research data.

Books and publishing

Choose named formats such as B1, B2, B3, B4, A4, and US Letter, or use book-oriented page presets. Export a finished PDF for publishing or self-publishing, including workflows where you are preparing a book for Amazon KDP.

Can Wordleaf help me write a book?

Yes, but it is not there to write the book instead of you. It helps you dictate, organize chapters and ideas, format the manuscript, work with images, build an outline from material you already have, and prepare the finished pages.

Is Wordleaf an AI writer or essay writer?

It works differently from tools that generate a replacement draft. Wordleaf can help organize and format your writing, understand document instructions, and work with the text you provide. It is specifically told not to invent missing information, sources, story beats, or answers unless you explicitly ask for new content.

Can it help with a Hero's Journey or 27-point story structure?

Yes. Give Wordleaf your scenes, notes, events, or ideas and it can help map them to the structure, show what you already have, and show where there are gaps. The empty parts remain yours to answer.

Can I use Wordleaf for a children's book or picture-heavy book?

You can add images, move them, change their placement, and work with text and pictures in the same project. Wordleaf helps with the document and layout; it does not invent the children's story for you.

Can I move a book from Word or Google Docs into Wordleaf?

Yes. Wordleaf can import Microsoft Word documents, Google Docs exports, plain text, and Markdown, so you can continue a project that started somewhere else.

Can Wordleaf help format a book for Amazon KDP?

Wordleaf can prepare and export a finished PDF using book-oriented page presets and document settings. You still make the publishing decisions; Wordleaf handles the document formatting and export.

Wordleaf is completely free to use.
