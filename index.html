Emira

Your life is more than a list of things to get done.

Emira is a native iPhone and iPad life calendar where you can set goals, keep memories, journal, and play relaxing games.

A quiet place to keep track of the life you are already living, without turning it into a productivity score.

Native iOS app

iPhone + iPad Coming soon to iOS

Life Calendar

See your life in weeks, months, and years. You choose the life-expectancy frame yourself. It gives the calendar a shape, but Emira does not turn it into a death countdown or tell you how much time you have left.

Eventually + Goals

Eventually can hold as many someday ideas as you want. Monthly Goals stay intentionally small: up to three at a time, including zero. Goals can change, go back to Eventually, be completed, or be released without being treated like failures.

Milestones + Journal

Milestones keep things that happened and mattered, including ordinary memories, not only achievements. Journal pages can cover one day or a range of days, include photos, connect to Milestones, and be reread in Book View.

What Emira is for

The Life Calendar is the center of Emira. It lets you see your life laid out as time instead of only looking at one day or one month at a time. Around it are places for things you might want to do, things that already happened, things you want to write down, and things you want to leave for yourself later. Planning is part of it, but so is remembering, looking back, changing your mind, or simply opening the app because you like being there.

Time Capsules + Reflection

Leave a message or photo for your future self, then let it stay closed until the date you chose. Reflection Reviews are occasional invitations to remember what happened, not checkups on whether you completed enough goals.

Little relaxing games

The Games area gives you something to do that is not planning. Memory, tracing, balancing, building, visual tracking, breathing, puzzles, and other quiet activities can have challenge without turning into scores, streaks, leaderboards, or pressure.

Make it feel like yours

Emira has color profiles, app icons, backgrounds, ambient music, sounds, and motion settings. You can also hide major parts of the app you do not use. The idea is to make Emira feel like somewhere you want to come back to, not a dashboard you have to maintain.

No pressure attached

There are no streaks to protect, overdue goals to clear, productivity scores, required daily check-ins, or reports grading how well you lived your month. Having no goals is normal. Ignoring a reflection is fine. Not journaling for three months does not create three months of empty boxes you now have to fill.

If something stops mattering, you can release it. If you only want the Life Calendar, use the Life Calendar. If you open Emira and do absolutely nothing, that is also allowed.

Private without pretending to know you

Emira does not use AI to analyze your Journal, goals, memories, photos, or mood. Its words are written on purpose, but it does not pretend those words mean it knows what you are feeling. Photos you choose for Emira stay with the app's saved content, and the iOS photo picker lets you choose what to share instead of giving the app your whole library.

Sometimes you just want somewhere quiet

Not every time you open Emira needs a purpose. You can look back through old pages, sit with the Life Calendar, change the colors, watch a slow background, play something for a minute, or leave the ambient music on while you do something else.

Emira isn’t here to tell you how to spend your time. It’s just a place to keep some of it.

Frequently asked questions

What does Emira actually do?

Life Calendar and time
What is the Life Calendar?

It is a visual view of your life laid out as time. Filled spaces represent time you have already lived, and the calendar keeps filling in as time passes. You can look at your life at different scales instead of only seeing a normal month calendar.

Is it a countdown to death?

No. Emira does not show a giant days-remaining number or try to scare you into being productive. The future part of the calendar is a visual frame around the life you have lived so far, not a prediction of when you will die.

Why does it ask for a life expectancy?

Because the calendar needs an ending point to draw the full shape. You choose that number yourself. It is a display setting, not a medical estimate and not Emira telling you how long you have.

Can I just use the Life Calendar and ignore the rest?

Yes. The Life Calendar can be the whole app for you if that is what you want. The other areas exist around it, but none of them are required.

Does Emira have widgets?

Yes. Widgets are meant to give you a small view of Emira from the Home Screen without turning that view into a reminder that you are behind on something.

Goals and Eventually
What is Eventually?

Eventually is where things can exist without a deadline. A place you want to go, something you want to learn, a project, a tiny idea, whatever. Putting something there does not mean you promised to do it.

Why can I only have three Monthly Goals?

Because a giant goal list stops feeling helpful pretty fast. Emira keeps the current month small on purpose. You can choose up to three things to keep close right now, while everything else can stay in Eventually without disappearing.

Can I have zero goals?

Yes. Zero is a normal number of goals. Emira is not going to fill the empty space with warnings or tell you to get back on track. Sometimes this month is just open.

What happens if I change my mind about a goal?

Change it. A goal can be kept, completed, returned to Eventually, or released. Releasing something is not the app deciding you failed. It just means you do not want to carry that thing anymore.

What if I do not finish a goal by the end of the month?

Nothing bad happens. There is no overdue state and no red failure badge. You can keep it, move it back to Eventually, change it, finish it later, or let it go.

Does Emira track streaks or completion rates?

No. It can remember what you chose and what happened, but it does not turn that history into a score about you. The point is to help you keep things in view, not calculate whether you are a good user.

Milestones and memories
What is a Milestone?

A Milestone is something that happened that you want to keep. It can be important, funny, small, personal, an achievement, a memory, or just a moment you know you will want to find again later.

Does a Milestone have to be an achievement?

No. That would miss the point. A life is not only the things you accomplished. Milestones can hold ordinary memories and meaningful moments too.

Can Milestones have photos and notes?

Yes. You can keep the date, write what you want to remember, and attach photos so the memory is more than a title on a timeline.

Can a completed goal become a Milestone?

Yes. If something you finished is worth remembering, it can move from being a thing you planned to being part of the record of what actually happened.

Can I add a future Milestone?

Milestones are for things that already happened. If something is still in the future, it makes more sense in Eventually or Monthly Goals until it becomes part of your actual history.

Journal and looking back
Do I have to journal every day?

No. There is no empty calendar full of blank days waiting to make you feel guilty. The Journal shows the pages you actually made.

Can a Journal entry cover more than one day?

Yes. A page can be about one day or a range of days. Some things make more sense as a weekend, a trip, a week, or a stretch of life instead of one exact date.

Can I add photos to Journal pages?

Yes. Journal pages can keep photos with the writing so you can remember what something looked like, not just what you wrote about it.

What is Book View?

Book View is for rereading. Instead of treating old entries like database rows, it gives you a more natural way to move through pages you have already written.

What is Year in Review?

It is a way to look back at a year through the things you actually saved, like Milestones, Journal pages, and photos. It is not a yearly performance report and it does not grade how much you accomplished.

Time Capsules and reflection
What is a Time Capsule?

A Time Capsule is something you leave for your future self. You can write a message, optionally add a photo, pick a future date, and leave it alone until then.

Can I see what is inside a Time Capsule before it opens?

The point is that you are not supposed to. The message and photo stay hidden until the opening date so it can actually feel like something your past self left for you.

What are Reflection Reviews?

They are occasional invitations to look back and ask whether anything happened that you would like to remember. They are not check-ins about whether you hit your goals.

What if I ignore a Reflection Review?

Nothing. It is an invitation, not an assignment. Emira does not keep a missed-review count or nag you to make it up later.

Why separate reflection from goals?

Because something can matter even if you never planned it. Reflection is there to catch parts of life that would disappear if the app only remembered what was on a goal list.

Games and quiet activities
Why does a life calendar have games?

Because not every useful minute inside an app needs to be planning or writing. The games are there as small things to do with your hands and attention when you want a quieter kind of interaction.

Are the games competitive?

No. They are not built around leaderboards, streaks, ranks, timers, lives, or a big Game Over screen. Some have challenge, memory, balance, or physics, but the tone is still calm.

What kinds of games are in Emira?

The Games area is built around simple ideas like breathing, watching motion, memory, visual tracking, balancing, tracing, building with physics, sliding-image puzzles, matching, and sound. The exact visible set can change while Emira is still being developed and tested.

Do the games connect to productivity or goals?

No. Playing a game does not earn points for your life, maintain a streak, unlock a productivity badge, or prove you had a good day. They are allowed to just be games.

Are there calmer alternatives to fast motion?

Yes. Reduced-motion behavior is part of Emira's design, and the activities are supposed to preserve their meaning without requiring a lot of animation just for the sake of animation.

Why do the games use sound and haptics?

Because touch can feel better when the app answers back a little. Emira uses sound and haptics as quiet feedback, not as casino-style rewards or constant attention-grabbing effects.

Colors, themes, backgrounds, and sound
How much can I change how Emira looks?

A lot. Emira has multiple color profiles, app icons, backgrounds, and appearance choices. The idea is not just to swap one accent color. A profile can change the feel of the whole app while keeping the interface readable and consistent.

Why are there so many color choices?

Because color changes the mood of the app more than people give it credit for. Forest green, ocean blues, indigo, midnight tones, tulip and rose colors, warmer dawn colors, sunflower tones, and softer variations can make the same screens feel completely different.

Do themes change how the app works?

No. The colors can change, but the meaning of the interface should stay stable. A button should not suddenly mean something different because you picked another profile.

What are the backgrounds like?

They are meant to sit behind the app instead of fighting with it. Some are photographic or illustrated, some are more abstract or procedural, and motion is kept slow enough that the background still feels like a background.

Does Emira have music?

Yes. Ambient music is part of the quiet side of Emira. It can keep playing while you write, read, use another app, or put the phone down, including background and Lock Screen playback where supported.

Why does the interface feel rounded and spacious?

That is intentional. Emira avoids hard, crowded surfaces where it can. Cards, pills, spacing, slow motion, and the larger color system are all part of making the app feel softer without making it childish.

Emira's voice and the no-AI rule
Is Emira an AI?

No. Emira does not have an AI assistant watching what you write or trying to interpret your life. The app's words are intentionally written rather than generated for you.

Then why does Emira have a personality?

Because software still talks to you. Button labels, empty states, help text, reminders, and little responses all have a tone whether you plan one or not. Emira's voice is written to be warm, quiet, direct, and not weirdly corporate.

Does Emira analyze how I am feeling?

No. It does not pretend to know why you stopped using the app, why you changed a goal, or what kind of month you are having. Rest, grief, burnout, boredom, a crisis, or just changing your mind can all look like inactivity from the outside. Emira does not guess.

Why does Emira avoid words like failed, overdue, and behind?

Because those words turn ordinary changes in a life into a judgment. If a goal stopped mattering, the useful information is that it changed. Emira does not need to add a moral score to it.

Will Emira motivate me or push me to catch up?

No. It can remind you about things you intentionally chose to be reminded about, but the app is not built around pressure. It should not make you feel like opening it created another person you have to satisfy.

Privacy, photos, and your data
Does Emira send my Journal or goals to an AI service?

No. There is no AI feature analyzing your Journal, goals, memories, or photos. That is a product rule, not just a setting you have to remember to turn off.

Where are my photos kept?

Photos you add to Emira are kept with the app's local content. Milestones, Journal pages, and Time Capsules can keep their own photos, and Time Capsule images stay hidden with the capsule until it opens.

Does Emira need my whole photo library?

No. The iOS photo picker lets you choose the photos you want to share with Emira instead of handing the app broad access to everything in your library.

When does Emira use the camera?

Only when you choose to take a photo from inside the app. The camera is not running in the background and opening Emira does not automatically turn it on.

Can I back up and restore Emira?

Emira includes backup and restore so the things you have saved are not trapped in one installation forever. The backup is meant to carry the app's own saved content, including its attached local photos.

What happens to attached photos if I remove something?

Photos belong to the Emira item they were added to. If you deliberately remove that saved item, its local attached photos can be removed with it instead of leaving orphaned copies inside the app.

Accessibility and control
Does Emira support Reduced Motion?

Yes. Motion is part of the look of Emira, but it should not be required to understand or use the app. Reduced Motion is supposed to keep the same information and controls with less movement.

Does Emira work with VoiceOver?

Accessibility is part of the app design, including meaningful labels and equivalent controls where a visual gesture would not translate well. The goal is the same function, not just describing a screen that is otherwise unusable.

Can I hide parts of Emira?

Yes. If you do not want Life, Goals, Milestones, Journal, or Games in your normal view, you can keep the app focused on the parts you actually use.

Does hiding a section delete what is in it?

No. Hiding something is a visibility choice. It is not supposed to quietly erase your Journal pages, memories, or other saved content.

What iOS version does Emira support?

The current native app targets iOS 17 or later.

Price, platform, and the name
Will Emira have a subscription?

No. The plan is one purchase. No subscription and no in-app purchases.

Is Emira a native iPhone app?

Yes. Emira is built as a native iOS app rather than a website wrapped inside an app. The interface, widgets, photo picker, notifications, accessibility behavior, and other system features are built around iOS.

Is Emira finished?

It is still being developed and tested. The core idea is settled, but individual screens, activities, wording, and polish can still change before the public release.

Why is it called Emira?

The name came from playing with the sound of mirror and Mira. The path was basically “Emira, E Mira, Mira, Mirra, Mirror.” The extra vowel made the name softer and let it flow into the rest of the word without the harder opening sound.

Is “Life's mirror” the official slogan?

Not right now. It is one of the ideas that came out of the name and the reflection theme, but Emira does not currently have a locked final slogan.

The Word Bank stores spellings and preferred terms you want Wordleaf to preserve. Writing Rules are instructions you explicitly tell Wordleaf to remember for the project. They are used as author preferences when Wordleaf interprets dictation or changes the document.

Story and fiction structures
What fiction and story structures does Wordleaf know?

Wordleaf can help organize your existing material with common structures including three-act structure, five-act structure, Freytag’s Pyramid, the Hero’s Journey, seven-point story structure, 27-point or 27-chapter planning, Story Circle, Story Spine, Save the Cat beat-sheet concepts, Kishōtenketsu, scene-sequel organization, the Fichtean Curve, the Snowflake Method, in medias res, frame narrative, nested narrative, parallel narrative, nonlinear narrative, episodic structure, circular structure, quest structure, mystery/reveal structure, romance arcs, tragedy arcs, character arcs, and coming-of-age arcs. These are references for sorting what you already have. If a framework has an empty slot, Wordleaf leaves it open for you instead of filling it with a new plot point.

What are the three-act and five-act structures?

Three-act structure: Act I establishes the situation and disruption; Act II develops complications, pressure, and a midpoint change; Act III brings the final confrontation and resolution. Five-act structure: exposition or setup; rising action; climax or major turning point; falling action or consequences; resolution or denouement. Wordleaf can map your scenes and notes to these parts and show what is still undecided.

What are the stages of the Hero’s Journey?

A common twelve-stage version uses: Ordinary World; Call to Adventure; Refusal of the Call; Meeting the Mentor; Crossing the First Threshold; Tests, Allies, and Enemies; Approach to the Inmost Cave; Ordeal; Reward; Road Back; Resurrection; and Return with the Elixir. Wordleaf can compare your existing scenes, events, and character changes with those stages. If a stage is empty, it points out the gap rather than inventing the event.

What is the seven-point story structure?

The common seven points are: Hook; Plot Turn 1; Pinch Point 1; Midpoint; Pinch Point 2; Plot Turn 2; and Resolution. Wordleaf can place your existing scenes or notes against those points and help you test the order without filling an empty point with a new plot beat.

What is the 27-point or 27-chapter story structure?

There is more than one 27-point variant, so Wordleaf does not pretend there is one universal set of named beats. By default it treats the method as a flexible three-act planning grid with nine blocks and three beats per block. If you are following a particular 27-point method, you can give Wordleaf that version and it will organize your existing material against it. Empty beats stay empty until you decide what belongs there.

What are the eight Story Circle steps?

The Story Circle is commonly summarized as: You; Need; Go; Search; Find; Take; Return; Change. Wordleaf can map your existing material to those eight steps and show where scenes or notes already fit.

What is the Story Spine?

A common Story Spine uses: Once upon a time; Every day; Until one day; Because of that; Because of that; Because of that; Until finally; Ever since then. The repeated “Because of that” section can expand as needed. Wordleaf can use the pattern to organize your own events without creating the missing events for you.

What are the Save the Cat beats?

The standard fifteen beat-sheet labels are: Opening Image; Theme Stated; Setup; Catalyst; Debate; Break into Two; B Story; Fun and Games; Midpoint; Bad Guys Close In; All Is Lost; Dark Night of the Soul; Break into Three; Finale; and Final Image. Wordleaf can help place material you already wrote or planned into those beats, but it does not automatically generate a Catalyst, Midpoint, Finale, or other missing beat.

What is Kishōtenketsu?

Kishōtenketsu uses four broad parts: Ki, introduction; Shō, development; Ten, twist or change; and Ketsu, reconciliation or conclusion. Wordleaf can help organize material this way without forcing a conflict-centered Western plot structure onto it.

What is scene-sequel structure?

A common scene-sequel model uses a scene of Goal, Conflict, and Disaster followed by a sequel of Reaction, Dilemma, and Decision. Wordleaf can help label and order material you already have using those functions without inventing the character’s goal, disaster, dilemma, or decision.

What is the Snowflake Method and can Wordleaf help with it?

The Snowflake Method is a planning method rather than one fixed plot beat sheet. It progressively expands a short premise into a synopsis, character material, scene planning, and increasingly detailed draft preparation. Wordleaf can help organize and expand the material you provide through those stages, but it should not choose the premise, characters, conflicts, or missing story decisions for you.

What nonfiction book structures can Wordleaf help organize?

It works with the facts, experiences, examples, and chapter material you provide instead of manufacturing missing expertise.

Clean carefully

Storage Guard separates disposable or rebuildable data from projects, documents, art, photos, saves, source code, databases, and anything else that may be user-created.

How Storage Guard decides what it can touch

It starts by reading, not guessing. Storage Guard can inspect the actual local file contents, headers, manifests, folder structure, timestamps, and application metadata instead of deciding from a filename alone.

Then it checks what is using the data. Before cleanup, Storage Guard asks Windows which applications or services currently have relevant files in use. Active data is a reason to keep it, not something to clean underneath a running program.

Safe does not automatically mean useful to delete. A browser cache may be disposable but still useful if that browser is used every day. Storage Guard also considers whether the owning app or data appears stale, whether the drive is actually under pressure, and whether cleaning it would meaningfully restore the configured guard.

Online cleanup knowledge is a soft authority. Vetted public cleanup definitions such as Winapp2 or BleachBit can strengthen the evidence that a particular cache, log, installer remnant, or other target is normally disposable. They never override Storage Guard's protected-data rules and never become permission by themselves.

Uncertain cases get challenged from both sides. Storage Guard uses separate Cleaner, Preserver, and Verifier review passes. The Cleaner builds the strongest evidence-based case for cleanup. The Preserver looks for user data, active application state, project material, or anything difficult to reproduce. The Verifier checks both against hard evidence and has the final veto.

The final rule is fail-closed. Projects, documents, artwork, photos, video, music, source repositories, game saves, databases, archives, and unknown data are never automatically deleted. If Storage Guard cannot prove that something is transient or reproducible, it keeps it or asks. AI or classifier confidence can help investigate an uncertain item, but it cannot grant deletion permission.

Storage Guard is designed to keep inspection local and conservative: read the evidence, check usage, challenge the conclusion, then act only when the final safety gate agrees.
