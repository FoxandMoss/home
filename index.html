Emira

Your life is more than a list of things to get done.

In short: Emira is a calm life calendar where you can set a few goals, keep memories, journal, and play little relaxing games.

It is made for reflection as much as planning. You can look at the life you have already lived, keep a few things you want to do close by, save what mattered, or open the app and do absolutely nothing productive.

There are no streaks to maintain, overdue goals to catch up on, or productivity scores judging how well you are living.

See your life

View your life as a growing calendar, choose your own life-expectancy estimate, and keep a quiet glimpse of it on your Home Screen with widgets.

Keep what matters

Use Eventually, monthly goals, Milestones, Journal pages, photos, Time Capsules, and gentle reflection tools without turning every memory into a task.

No pressure attached

Zero goals is valid. Releasing a goal is valid. Rest is valid. Emira does not grade, rank, analyze, or punish you for how you spend your time.

Emira is not here to tell you how to spend your time. It is just a place to keep some of it.

What Emira is
What is Emira?

Emira is a life calendar and reflection app. It gives you one place to see the time you have lived, keep a few goals nearby, save memories and Milestones, journal, leave Time Capsules for yourself, and spend a little time with calm games and activities.

Is Emira a productivity app?

No. Emira can help you keep track of things you would like to do, but it is deliberately not built around output, efficiency, streaks, overdue work, rankings, or productivity scores. Planning is one part of the app, not the purpose of your life.

Do I have to use Emira every day?

No. There are no required daily check-ins and nothing bad happens when you do not open it. Emira is designed to still make sense after a quiet day, a quiet month, or a long time away.

What if I open Emira and do not want to plan anything?

That is normal. You can look at your Life Calendar, reread something you wrote, listen to the ambient music, watch a background move, play a quiet activity, or simply close the app again. Not every visit needs a purpose.

Does Emira tell me how I should live?

No. It does not assign a correct number of goals, judge how much you accomplished, or tell you that you are behind. It is meant to hold parts of your life, not score them.

Life Calendar and time
What is the Life Calendar?

The Life Calendar is a visual view of your life over time. Filled areas represent time you have already lived, while future time sits inside the life-expectancy estimate you choose for yourself.

Is the Life Calendar a countdown to death?

No. Emira does not show a dramatic “days remaining” countdown. The point is to make time visible without turning it into a threat or deadline.

Can I choose my own life-expectancy estimate?

Yes. The estimate belongs to you and is used only to shape the future portion of the Life Calendar. It is not a medical prediction.

Can I see my life in different scales?

Yes. Emira is built around looking at life at more than one scale, including week and year views, so you can move between the detail of recent time and the larger shape of a life.

Can the Life Calendar appear outside the app?

Yes. Emira includes widgets so you can keep a small view of your Life Calendar on your Home Screen without opening the full app.

Eventually, goals, and prompts
What is Eventually?

Eventually is an unlimited place for things you might want to do someday. They do not need dates or deadlines, and putting something there does not turn it into an obligation.

How many monthly goals can I have?

Up to three active goals in a month. You can also have one, two, or none. “This month is open” is a complete and acceptable state.

What happens if I change my mind about a goal?

You can keep it, return it to Eventually, complete it, or release it. Releasing something is treated as a valid change of mind, not a failure.

Does Emira use overdue labels or missed-goal warnings?

No. Emira intentionally avoids language such as overdue, failed, abandoned, behind, or unproductive when talking about your life and goals.

Can Emira give me ideas when I do not know what to add?

Yes. Optional prompt strips can help you think of goals or Milestones without deciding for you. They are suggestions you can browse, ignore, or use as a starting point.

What kinds of prompt categories are there?

The current goal prompt library is organized around People, Wellbeing, Outdoors, Crafting, Visual Art, Music & Sound, Video, Writing, Explore, Curiosity & Reading, Home, Play, Work, and General, with an All view that brings the categories together.

Memories, Milestones, and Journal
What is a Milestone?

A Milestone is something from your life that feels worth keeping. It can be an achievement, a memory, a change, a small moment, or something meaningful that would never belong on a résumé.

Can a completed goal become a Milestone?

Yes. When a goal turns into something you want to remember, it can become part of your life record instead of disappearing into a completed-task list.

Do Milestones have to be achievements?

No. The point is what mattered to you, not whether it looks impressive. Ordinary memories and personal moments belong there too.

How does the Journal work?

You can write about one day or a range of days, add photos, connect entries with Milestones, and flip through existing pages in a book-like view.

Does the Journal show empty days that I failed to fill?

No. The Journal lists pages that actually exist. It does not create a calendar full of blank boxes waiting to become guilt.

Can I hide the Journal if I do not use it?

Yes. Emira is designed so major areas can be hidden without deleting the content you already saved. You can keep only the parts of the app that are useful to you.

Time Capsules and looking back
What is a Time Capsule?

A Time Capsule is a message for your future self. You can include a message, optionally add a photo, and choose a future date before it becomes available.

Can I peek inside a Time Capsule early?

Emira is designed not to reveal the message or photo before the opening date. When the time comes, the idea is simple: you left something for yourself.

What are Reflection Reviews?

They are gentle, optional invitations to look back every few months. They are meant to help you notice what happened, not review your performance.

What is Year in Review?

Year in Review lets you choose a year and revisit its Milestones, Journal pages, memories, and photos. It is a record of what happened, not a productivity report.

Can I remember something without making it a goal?

Yes. Emira keeps planning and remembering separate on purpose. Something can matter without becoming a task.

Games and quiet activities
Why does a life-calendar app have games?

Because sometimes you want a place to be for a minute without organizing anything. The games are small, calm activities that give Emira somewhere to go when planning or writing is not what you need.

Do the games have scores, ranks, streaks, or Game Over screens?

No. Emira’s game design avoids score pressure, leaderboards, competitive ranking, streak requirements, and punishment states. The interaction itself is the point.

What kinds of activities are in Emira?

The game library explores breathing, passive visual meditation, memory, observation, balancing, physical structure building, line tracing, image restoration, matching, and sound-and-shape interaction. Individual activities can appear as they reach the quality bar for the public library.

Are the activities supposed to be difficult?

They can still ask for attention, memory, balance, or coordination, but they are not built around making you feel punished for getting something wrong.

Do games use the same look as the rest of Emira?

Yes. Each activity can have its own identity, but it still belongs to Emira through the same themes, accessibility rules, restrained feedback, rounded surfaces, sound philosophy, and calm visual language.

Can I hide Games?

Yes. Games are optional, just like the other major sections. If you do not want them in your everyday app, you can hide them.

Colors, themes, icons, and backgrounds
How customizable is Emira?

Very. Emira is built so the app can feel visually personal without changing its underlying behavior. Themes affect the page, cards, controls, calendar, widgets, games, and other Emira-owned surfaces as one coherent system.

How many color themes are there?

The current color system has ten theme families: Forest, Mint, Ocean, Indigo, Midnight, Tulip, Rose, Dawn, Sunflower, and Mono.

What are the 50 color profiles?

Each of the ten theme families has five appearances: Tinted Light, Tinted Medium, Tinted Dark, System Light, and System Dark. Together that makes 50 coordinated color profiles.

Does changing the theme only recolor one accent?

No. Emira’s theme system is designed as a full palette, not a single accent picker. Backgrounds, cards, controls, text, calendar states, widgets, games, and decorative elements resolve through the active profile so the app stays visually coherent.

Can I choose different app icons and backgrounds?

Yes. Emira includes alternate app icons and multiple background choices, including quiet visual backgrounds that can move slowly rather than demanding attention.

Can I hide parts of the app?

Yes. The Life Calendar, Goals, Milestones, Journal, and Games can be hidden when you do not want them. Emira is meant to adapt to what you actually use.

Music, motion, and accessibility
Does Emira have music?

Yes. Emira includes original ambient music for quiet reading, writing, reflection, or simply leaving the phone nearby for a while.

Can the music keep playing when I leave the app?

Yes. The music system is designed for background and Lock Screen playback so it can continue while you read, write, use another app, or put the phone down.

Does Emira use sound effects?

Yes, but the goal is restrained feedback rather than noisy gamification. Small interactions can have soft, satisfying sounds while ambient music remains its own separate layer.

What if I am sensitive to motion?

Emira includes reduced-motion support. Decorative movement and activity behavior are designed to have calmer equivalents rather than making motion a requirement for understanding the app.

Is Emira being designed for VoiceOver and larger text?

Yes. Accessibility is part of the app’s design contracts, including meaningful labels, usable hit targets, contrast checks, reduced-motion equivalents, and layouts that are meant to adapt instead of assuming one screen size.

Privacy, photos, AI, and backups
Does Emira use AI to analyze my life?

No. Emira has no AI feature that reads your journal, evaluates your goals, guesses your mood, or tells you what your life means.

Is the personality in Emira an AI assistant?

No. Emira has an intentionally written app voice, but it is not pretending to be a person watching you. The words in the app are authored to feel quiet, warm, spacious, and human without claiming to know what you feel.

Where are photos stored?

Photos you add to Emira are stored privately with the app on your device. The photo picker lets you choose what to share with Emira rather than asking for broad access to your whole library.

Can I take a photo inside Emira?

Yes. Where photo attachments are supported, you can choose an existing photo or use the camera after explicitly tapping the camera option.

Can I back up my Emira data?

Yes. Emira includes backup and restore support, and its backup is designed to keep the data and attached photos needed to rebuild your saved content.

What happens to attached photos if I remove an entry?

When an item such as a Milestone, Journal entry, or Time Capsule is released or removed, its locally stored photo attachments are removed with it rather than being left behind as orphaned app data.

Price, devices, and availability
Will Emira have a subscription?

No. Emira is designed around a one-time purchase rather than an ongoing subscription.

Will Emira have in-app purchases?

No. The product direction is one purchase with no in-app purchases.

What devices is Emira made for?

Emira is a native SwiftUI app for iOS 17 and later, with layouts for iPhone and iPad rather than a web wrapper or cross-platform shell.

Does Emira need a browser or account to be useful?

No. The core app is designed as a native personal space on your device, not as a website you have to keep signed into.

The name and the feeling of Emira
Why is it called Emira?

The name grew out of the sound and idea of “mirror” and “Mira.” The softer “E” beginning felt better and matched the idea of a place that reflects a life back to you without judging it.

What is Emira supposed to feel like?

Quiet, warm, spacious, and unhurried. The colors, rounded surfaces, backgrounds, music, sounds, writing, and interactions are all meant to leave a little room between things.

Why does Emira avoid words like failed or overdue?

Because the app does not know why your plans changed. You might be resting, grieving, busy, burned out, sick, excited about something else, or simply different from the person who made the goal. Emira should not turn that uncertainty into blame.

Why are there so many ways to personalize it?

Because a life app can be very present on your phone. Themes, icons, backgrounds, music, hidden tabs, and motion choices let the same structure feel more like your own space instead of one fixed aesthetic imposed on everyone.

What is the simplest way to explain Emira?

It is a life calendar where you can set goals, keep memories, journal, and play little relaxing games.

Wordleaf

Write with your voice.

Writing should be free and accessible.

Wordleaf is a writing program built for people who like to talk through their work. You can dictate a paragraph, make a heading, move a picture, change the page layout, sort notes into an outline, or clean up the document without stopping to hunt through menus.

You can talk to it normally. It can tell when you are writing, quoting someone, thinking through an idea, or asking it to change the document. You can still type whenever you want, but the whole program is designed so your voice can do most of the work.

Speak or type

Dictate full passages, dialogue, notes, or rough ideas. Wordleaf can clean up obvious speech-to-text mess, add punctuation, keep quoted speech together, and remember spellings or terms you want it to preserve.

Format with your voice

Tell it to make a heading, start a list, add a page break, change margins or spacing, add page numbers, make columns, build a table of contents, move text, or change how part of the page is laid out.

Work with pictures and visuals

Add and place pictures, move them around the document, and work with tables, figures, charts, diagrams, timelines, equations, captions, references, and other visual or technical material.

Organize your outline

Bring in your scenes, notes, chapters, or ideas and sort them into structures such as the Hero’s Journey, Story Circle, Save the Cat, or a 27-point structure. Wordleaf can show what fits where, what is missing, and what may work better in a different order.

Essays, research, and nonfiction

Organize essays, reports, research papers, theses, literature reviews, citations, references, tables, and figures. It also has guidance for APA, MLA, Chicago/Turabian, IEEE, AMA, and common report formats.

Bring it in, finish it, export it

Import Word files, Google Docs exports, plain text, or Markdown. Choose a page format, preview the finished pages as you work, export a PDF, or save the full editable project so you can reopen it later.

Story and fiction structures
What fiction and story structures does Wordleaf know?

Wordleaf can help organize your existing material with common structures including three-act structure, five-act structure, Freytag’s Pyramid, the Hero’s Journey, seven-point story structure, 27-point or 27-chapter planning, Story Circle, Story Spine, Save the Cat beat-sheet concepts, Kishōtenketsu, scene-sequel organization, the Fichtean Curve, the Snowflake Method, in medias res, frame narrative, nested narrative, parallel narrative, nonlinear narrative, episodic structure, circular structure, quest structure, mystery/reveal structure, romance arcs, tragedy arcs, character arcs, and coming-of-age arcs. These are references for sorting what you already have. If a framework has an empty slot, Wordleaf leaves it open for you instead of filling it with a new plot point.

What are the three-act and five-act structures?

Three-act structure: Act I establishes the situation and disruption; Act II develops complications, pressure, and a midpoint change; Act III brings the final confrontation and resolution. Five-act structure: exposition or setup; rising action; climax or major turning point; falling action or consequences; resolution or denouement. Wordleaf can map your scenes and notes to these parts and show what is still undecided.

What are the stages of the Hero’s Journey?

A common twelve-stage version uses: Ordinary World; Call to Adventure; Refusal of the Call; Meeting the Mentor; Crossing the First Threshold; Tests, Allies, and Enemies; Approach to the Inmost Cave; Ordeal; Reward; Road Back; Resurrection; and Return with the Elixir. Wordleaf can compare your existing scenes, events, and character changes with those stages. If a stage is empty, it points out the gap rather than inventing the event.

What is the seven-point story structure?

The common seven points are: Hook; Plot Turn 1; Pinch Point 1; Midpoint; Pinch Point 2; Plot Turn 2; and Resolution. Wordleaf can place your existing scenes or notes against those points and help you test the order without filling an empty point with a new plot beat.

What is the 27-point or 27-chapter story structure?

There is more than one 27-point variant, so Wordleaf does not pretend there is one universal set of named beats. By default it treats the method as a flexible three-act planning grid with nine blocks and three beats per block. If you are following a particular 27-point method, you can give Wordleaf that version and it will organize your existing material against it. Empty beats stay empty until you decide what belongs there.

What are the eight Story Circle steps?

The Story Circle is commonly summarized as: You; Need; Go; Search; Find; Take; Return; Change. Wordleaf can map your existing material to those eight steps and show where scenes or notes already fit.

What is the Story Spine?

A common Story Spine uses: Once upon a time; Every day; Until one day; Because of that; Because of that; Because of that; Until finally; Ever since then. The repeated “Because of that” section can expand as needed. Wordleaf can use the pattern to organize your own events without creating the missing events for you.

What are the Save the Cat beats?

The standard fifteen beat-sheet labels are: Opening Image; Theme Stated; Setup; Catalyst; Debate; Break into Two; B Story; Fun and Games; Midpoint; Bad Guys Close In; All Is Lost; Dark Night of the Soul; Break into Three; Finale; and Final Image. Wordleaf can help place material you already wrote or planned into those beats, but it does not automatically generate a Catalyst, Midpoint, Finale, or other missing beat.

What is Kishōtenketsu?

Kishōtenketsu uses four broad parts: Ki, introduction; Shō, development; Ten, twist or change; and Ketsu, reconciliation or conclusion. Wordleaf can help organize material this way without forcing a conflict-centered Western plot structure onto it.

What is scene-sequel structure?

A common scene-sequel model uses a scene of Goal, Conflict, and Disaster followed by a sequel of Reaction, Dilemma, and Decision. Wordleaf can help label and order material you already have using those functions without inventing the character’s goal, disaster, dilemma, or decision.

What is the Snowflake Method and can Wordleaf help with it?

The Snowflake Method is a planning method rather than one fixed plot beat sheet. It progressively expands a short premise into a synopsis, character material, scene planning, and increasingly detailed draft preparation. Wordleaf can help organize and expand the material you provide through those stages, but it should not choose the premise, characters, conflicts, or missing story decisions for you.

Can Wordleaf help organize a memoir or biography?

Yes. It can help sort memories, events, interviews, notes, research, and chapters by time, theme, place, person, or another pattern you choose. For memoir, that can mean moving between scenes and reflection. For biography, it can help keep periods of a person’s life and the research attached to them in a clearer order.

Can Wordleaf help make a how-to book, guide, or manual?

Yes. It can help organize instructions into prerequisites, materials, steps, warnings, examples, troubleshooting, and reference sections. It can also help keep headings and repeated section formats consistent so a guide or manual is easier to follow.

Can Wordleaf help with reports, proposals, resumes, or letters?

Yes. Wordleaf can organize and format business reports, proposals, resumes and CVs, letters, technical documents, lab reports, notes, and similar projects. It can help with headings, sections, lists, tables, page layout, spacing, and consistent formatting across the document.

Yes. Wordleaf has an image library for local PNG, JPG, JPEG, WebP, GIF, and SVG files. Images can be inserted into the document and included in PDF export. Imports, projects, backups, and file types
Can I make a book in Wordleaf instead of Microsoft Word?

Yes. Wordleaf is intended for book-length projects and combines dictation, organization, document formatting, images, page layout, live PDF preview, project saving, and PDF export. If you already started in Microsoft Word, you can import a .docx file and continue working in Wordleaf.

Can I move a Google Docs book into Wordleaf?

Yes. Export the Google Docs document as a Word .docx file and import that file into Wordleaf. Wordleaf also accepts plain-text and Markdown files. This lets you continue a manuscript that began in Google Docs without copying every section by hand.

Exactly what files can Wordleaf import?

Editable writing imports currently include Microsoft Word .docx, Google Docs exported as .docx, plain text .txt, Markdown .md, native Wordleaf project files, and compatible project folders. Image assets can be added as PNG, JPG, JPEG, WebP, GIF, or SVG. Wordleaf does not currently advertise direct PDF-to-editable-document import, direct .doc export, direct .rtf import, or direct Google Docs cloud-account import.

Can Wordleaf import a PDF as editable writing?

Not currently. Wordleaf does not advertise direct PDF-to-editable-manuscript conversion. PDF files can exist as project assets in compatible project folders, but the normal editable-document import paths are Word .docx, Google Docs exported as .docx, plain text .txt, Markdown .md, native Wordleaf projects, and compatible project folders.

Can Wordleaf export a Word .docx file?

Not currently. The finished-document export is PDF, and the editable-project export is Wordleaf’s native project format. Plain-text or .docx export is not advertised until it exists as a finished export option.

What can Wordleaf export?

Wordleaf can export a finished PDF and can export the complete editable Wordleaf project so the manuscript, assets, settings, writing rules, and related project data can travel together and be reopened later. A separate plain-text export is not currently advertised.

Does Wordleaf keep backups?

Yes. Wordleaf supports local automatic manuscript backups every 15 minutes, every hour, or daily, plus manual backups and backup history. Backups can be restored from inside the program.

Publishing, PDFs, and page sizes
Exactly what page sizes can Wordleaf use?

Named formats currently include B1 (707 × 1000 mm), B2 (500 × 707 mm), B3 (353 × 500 mm), B4 (250 × 353 mm), A4 (210 × 297 mm), and US Letter (8.5 × 11 in). Book-oriented presets currently include 5 × 8 in, 6 × 9 in, 6 × 9 in with bleed, 8 × 10 in, and 8.5 × 11 in. The selected page profile is used by the live preview and PDF export.

Can Wordleaf make a publishing-ready PDF?

Yes. Wordleaf has a live PDF workflow and can export the current project as a finished PDF using the selected page profile. You can control page size and ask for document formatting such as margins, headers, footers, page numbers, spacing, headings, images, tables, figures, and other layout details before exporting.

Can I use the PDF for Amazon KDP or Kindle Direct Publishing?

Wordleaf can create a finished PDF for a book workflow, including projects intended for Amazon Kindle Direct Publishing (KDP) or other print and self-publishing services that accept PDF interiors. Wordleaf prepares the document; you still choose the publisher’s trim size, bleed, margins, cover requirements, metadata, and upload settings and should check the current requirements of the publishing service before submission.

Can I change the page size, margins, or layout before I export?

Yes. You can change the page profile and ask for layout changes such as margins, spacing, headers, footers, page numbers, columns, image placement, and other formatting before exporting the finished PDF.

How Wordleaf works
Does Wordleaf rewrite my writing automatically?

No. It is built to preserve your meaning, voice, terminology, intentional fragments, dialect, and style. It may clean speech-recognition artifacts or reorganize material when you ask, but substantive rewriting is supposed to happen only when you explicitly request it.

Will Wordleaf fill in missing story beats or blank outline sections for me?

No, not by default. “Organize” means arrange, group, label, map, or move your existing material. If a Hero’s Journey stage, 27-point beat, essay section, research section, chapter, or placeholder is empty, Wordleaf can identify the gap and help you think about it, but it leaves that missing content for you unless you ask it to help create something new.

Is Wordleaf an AI writer?

Wordleaf includes local language tools to understand dictation, context, organization, and document instructions, but its design is not “press a button and replace the author.” It is built to work with your words and your ideas, make the smallest requested change, and avoid silently filling in missing content.

Can Wordleaf help me figure out how to write a book?

It can help you take what you already know about the book and make it easier to work with: collect notes, dictate ideas, group related material, build and revise an outline, compare your material with common story structures, format chapters, add images, and prepare the final document. It will not decide what your book should be about or invent the missing book for you.

Is Wordleaf free?

Yes. Wordleaf is completely free to use. The Windows download is available now. A macOS build is planned and will be added when there is a real tested Mac release to download.
